The widget at four fleet sizes

The first seven hosts are the ones reporting today, in the live priority order, with numbers from the panel capture. The rest are placeholders.

What changed from the live widget

  • Text sizes. Host name 28 bold (was 20). Uptime 20 in white (was 14). Labels 20 (were 14). CPU and GPU figures 36 bold (were 24). RAM figure 28 bold (was 16). Nothing on the widget is smaller than 20.
  • CPU on one line. Average in blue, then the top core in purple (#AB47BC), matching the two lines in the graph. The RAM figure takes the colour of the RAM bar. The separate "top core" row is gone.
  • Graphs. CPU is 321×130 (was 147×60). GPU is 321×70 (was 147×40) with a 4 px line.
  • Drives. One row: the fullest drive, by name, with its bar. Yellow at 80%, red at 90%, as now. If more drives are over the line, a "+1" follows the name. The row never moves.
  • Compact tiles. Same doubling: name 28 bold, uptime 20 on the same line, four bars with 20 px labels. The fill rule is unchanged (ten columns, three tiles each).
  • Colours and attention. Unchanged: border and band go yellow or red, and the row that caused it is drawn in the same colour.

Three things I chose for you

Say so if you want any of them the other way.

Rows sit at the same height in every column

A host with no GPU has a gap between RAM and the drive row. The alternative is a taller CPU graph on those hosts, which fills the gap but puts RAM at a different height from one column to the next.

OS name stays, on the uptime line

It is the one piece of text still in the muted colour. Dropping it would let uptime sit beside the host name and give the graphs another 30 px of height.

The empty columns are left empty here

With seven hosts, three columns are free: 1111×596, drawn as a dashed outline in the first figure. What goes there is the later brainstorm.
