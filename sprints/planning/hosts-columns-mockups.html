How it behaves

  • CPU graph. The filled blue area is average CPU; the thin sapphire line above it is the busiest single core. The big number is the average, the small one is the top core. Ten minutes of history, kept in memory on the panel.
  • Attention. Nothing changes size. A host that needs a look gets a yellow border, one that needs you now gets a red one, and the row that caused it (RAM, a drive) is drawn in the same colour. A host that stopped reporting gets a red border and says how long ago.
  • Thresholds. RAM and each drive: yellow at 80%, red at 90%. CPU, GPU and VRAM never raise attention; a loaded model keeps VRAM above 90% all day.
  • Priority. The order in KPIDASH_PRIORITY_CLIENTS, which the panel already reads. Hosts not in the list come after it, by name. The same order decides who goes compact first.
  • Compact tile. Name, uptime, then one bar each for CPU (with a tick at the top core), RAM, GPU if there is one, and the fullest drive.
  • Drives in a full column. Up to five, fullest first when there are more.

Two things I chose for you

Both are in the proposal as defaults. Say so if you want either the other way.

Asleep laptops are hidden

A host on the intermittent list that is not reporting takes no slot, so the fleet reflows when a laptop wakes. The switch above shows the alternative, where it keeps its slot and dims.

The other three cells of the top row stay empty for now

The widget takes columns 0 to 2 of row 0. Dev graphs, NAS and 10G stay where they are. Columns 3 to 5 of row 0 are left dark until you pick what goes there, one step at a time.
