Where the aircraft would come from

My pick: a free community feed now, your own receiver later

Sites such as airplanes.live and adsb.fi answer "every aircraft within N miles of this point" in one request, with no key, for personal use, at up to one request a second. One request every 10 seconds covers both aircraft cards. They promise no uptime, so the card needs a plain "no feed" state.

The step up is a $35 radio dongle and a small antenna on any Pi. It hears the aircraft directly, updates every second, costs nothing to run and depends on no one. The collector would read either source the same way, so starting with the free feed loses nothing.

FlightAware's key is the wrong tool for the map, and a good one for the detail

FlightAware charges per query, with $5 a month free and about ten queries a minute on the personal plan. Redrawing a map every 10 seconds is some 260,000 queries a month, so it cannot feed the map. What it has that the free feeds lack is the route: where a flight came from and where it is going. A few lookups an hour for the flight on final would fit inside the free allowance. I have not confirmed the exact price of each query type.

Ferries, tides and daylight are free

Washington State Ferries publishes live vessel positions for a free access code. Tide predictions for Seattle come from NOAA with no key. Sunrise, sunset and the moon are arithmetic on the Pi.

Things to know before picking

  • Movement. Aircraft would step to a new place every 10 seconds, with no animation between steps. The tide marker moves one pixel a minute. If stepping aircraft still bother you, a 30-second step is the fallback.
  • The coastline here is sketched by hand. The real card would bake it once from public map data, so it will look better than this.
  • Your location is not on the map. It is centred on the Sound, not on the house. A "nearest to home" marker is possible later, with the coordinates held outside any repo, as for the weather.
  • Map colours are height: peach is low, lavender is cruising. On the approach card height is the vertical axis, so colour shows climbing, level or descending.
  • The agents card needs a feed that does not exist yet. karc and ksitrep know the current state but keep no day-long history in Redis. It is the largest build of the four after the aircraft collector.
  • Aircraft counts, session names and tide heights on this page are made up for the picture.
