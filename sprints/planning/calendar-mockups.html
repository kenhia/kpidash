The four cards

How a day is marked

  • Day off: a blue-tinted background. Weekends get a plain grey one, so a weekday off stands out from a Saturday.
  • Holiday: the number in rose.
  • Special day: a yellow ring round the date. A birthday is the example; anything you tag as special gets it.
  • Today: a solid blue cell with dark text. It keeps its ring if it is also a special day.
  • They stack. The 27th is a day off and a birthday, so it has the tint and the ring. Card C marks the same three things with a bar, a colour and a dot, for comparison.
  • Days gone by are dimmed, so the eye lands on what is ahead.

Where the tagged days come from

Holidays: worked out by rule, no yearly chore

US federal holidays are all "a fixed date" or "the nth weekday of a month", so a few lines of date arithmetic give every year's list. Nothing to generate each January, and nothing to go stale. A holiday you do not want shown, or one that is not federal, goes in the list below.

Your days: one small list, one Redis record

Days off and special days live in one plain file, one line per entry, with a yearly flag for birthdays so Lynn's is entered once. A small publisher merges that list with the computed holidays and writes one record to the dashboard's Redis. Asking an agent to "add the 27th as a day off" is then a one-line edit.

2026-11-25  off
2026-11-27  off
11-27       special  Lynn's birthday   # no year: repeats

Where that file lives is the open choice. My pick is korg, as a small list it serves over its API, because you already reach korg from every machine and from your phone through the gate. The alternative is a file in a repo on kai.

The panel draws the month itself

The card works out the grid from the clock on the Pi and only reads the tagged days from Redis. If the record is missing, the calendar still shows, just with no tags.

Things to know before picking

  • The clock shows hours and minutes only. No seconds and no blinking colon, so nothing on the card moves more than once a minute.
  • The big clock needs a colon added to the 96 px digits font. That font was built for weather with digits only. Card D uses a size the dashboard already has.
  • Six-week months fit. The rows get shorter; press "A six-week month" to see August 2026. The August tags are made up for the picture.
  • Card B's cell labels are short. "Lynn", not "Lynn's birthday", so each tag carries an optional short name.
  • Colour: blue is new on this dashboard and is this card's accent. Rose and yellow are used here for holiday and special, not as warnings.
